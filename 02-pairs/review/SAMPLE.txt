C06, C12 and C10. Before the rebuild these carried four, four and two
differences. The audit of all twelve now shows one discriminator each.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY THESE THREE

C06 and C12 were the scaffolder's fault, not the author's. Their axis read
"is there a thrombosis risk factor AND sudden onset", which is two
discriminators in one line, so no amount of careful writing could produce a
clean set. The axis is now "did the pain come on suddenly or build gradually",
and THE RISK FACTOR IS HELD CONSTANT: the long flight is in both halves of C06,
the knee operation is in both halves of C12. The set now teaches onset speed.
Before, it could equally have taught "flight means red".

C10 differed in the PROFILE: the yellow half gained high blood pressure and
amlodipine, an independent cardiac risk factor that justified yellow on its own
and made the intended axis redundant. Both halves now have no cardiac history
and no medications. The profile still differs in one respect, the panic disorder
diagnosis, and that IS the axis. C04 is the same by design.

CORRECTION to an earlier version of this note, which said the diagnosis "cannot
be stated anywhere else". That was wrong: it is also stated in the symptom line,
"the same as the ones he has had for years". The profile and the symptom text
say the same thing twice, which is consistent rather than contradictory, but the
claim that the profile was the only possible place for it was false.

HOW TO READ EACH SET. The shared text is identical in both halves, character for
character, up to the point where they diverge. Read the shared block once, then
the two tails. If anything in the tails other than the named discriminator
differs, the set is still broken.
